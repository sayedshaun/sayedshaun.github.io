About

I got into machine learning through software engineering, and honestly that's still how I think about most problems. Training the model is rarely what trips people up. It's usually the pipeline nobody wrote down, a GPU bill nobody planned for, or noticing halfway through that your eval metric quietly stopped meaning anything a while back.

Right now that means building agentic systems at work: LLMs that call tools, talk to a few different databases, and actually hold up once real traffic hits them, not just in a demo. I spend a fair bit of time on the less glamorous half too: making all of that cheap enough to run. Outside of work I do research in low-resource NLP, and I'm gearing up for a master's to go further on both.
